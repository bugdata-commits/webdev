Technology should help us move forward.
Not forget what we depend on.

We want the technology we build and use to sit alongside care for the natural world. This page sets out the principles we are working toward—without claiming environmental results we have not yet measured.

What responsible technology means to us

Small choices matter. Evidence matters too.

Digital tools rely on devices, networks and energy. We aim to make choices that respect those costs while helping people access useful services and information.

⌁
Thoughtful design

Use less complexity where less is enough

Prioritise clear, accessible pages and maintainable systems. A right-sized solution can be easier to operate, improve and keep useful over time.

↻
Care for devices

Extend useful life

Where a project allows, support practices such as maintenance, repair, reuse and responsible recycling rather than unnecessary replacement.

✳
Nature and learning

Connect technology to local stewardship

Work with educators and community groups to make biodiversity and climate information easier to share, understand and act on.

How we will stay honest

Ambition, with accountability.

Environmental responsibility is ongoing work, not a badge. We intend to keep our language proportionate to what we can show.

  • Prefer specific, explainable actions over broad “green” claims.
  • Do not publish carbon savings, biodiversity gains or impact totals without a reliable basis.
  • Consider accessibility, data minimisation, device life and maintainability when shaping digital projects.
  • Listen to local organisations and experts before representing community or conservation work.
  • Review this page as our actions and evidence develop.
