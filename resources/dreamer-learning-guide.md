# The Motivated Dreamer
## Digital Skills Starter Guide · Bugdata Investments

**A practical first path for learners using a laptop or smartphone**

This guide is for learning and practice. It does not promise a job, clients, income or instant results. Skills grow through focused practice, honest feedback and work that solves a real problem. ## Start with what you have

You do not need to begin with expensive equipment. A phone or laptop, internet access where available, a way to write down tasks and a willingness to practise are enough to start exploring. Check whether each app is free, what its limits are and what information it collects before using it. Never share your passwords or one-time security codes with a client or tutor.

## Step 1 · Notice a real need

Look for a task that people or small organisations already spend time on: making a clear flyer, organising product information, preparing social posts, editing a short video, updating a basic webpage or turning rough notes into a readable document.

Ask three people:

- What task takes longer than it should?
- What do you currently do to solve it?
- What would make the result more useful?

Listen without promising that you can solve everything. Write down the words people use and separate what you heard from what you guessed.

## Step 2 · Choose one starter service

Pick a task that fits your current device, time and ability. A first service might be a simple flyer, a set of social content drafts, a short edited clip or a clean one-page web presence. Start with one clear deliverable instead of advertising every skill at once.

A practical checklist:

- Can I explain the deliverable in one sentence?
- Can I create a sample using tools I can access?
- Can I estimate the time and identify costs?
- Do I know which parts need the customer's approval?
- Can I deliver without using their private passwords or data?

## Step 3 · Make an honest sample

Create an original practice example. Use fictional names and information unless you have permission to use real material. Label it as a concept or sample. Do not copy another designer's work and present it as your own.

For a sample flyer, include a clear heading, one main message, readable contact details and one next step. For a short video, plan the opening, the main point, captions and a concise close. For a webpage, check that it works on a phone, has readable contrast and makes it easy to contact the business.

Save a screenshot or export, write down the tools you used and note what you would improve next time.

## Step 4 · Write a simple offer

Use this format:

> I help **[type of person or business]** with **[one task]** by delivering **[specific output]**. To begin, we agree the content, deadline, price and revision limit.

Before work starts, agree:

- what files or information the client must provide;
- exactly what you will deliver and in which format;
- the deadline and review dates;
- the included revision count and what counts as a new request;
- the price, payment timing and any outside costs;
- who owns or can use the final work, and whether you may show it in a portfolio.

Never promise guaranteed sales, followers, search rankings or income. Be clear about what you can and cannot do today.

## Step 5 · Find a first conversation respectfully

Make a small list of people or organisations who are genuinely relevant. A personal message can say what you noticed, offer one useful idea and ask whether they would like to talk. Do not send repeated bulk messages, scrape private contact details or pressure people who are not interested.

Example:

> Hello, I noticed your weekly class updates are shared as long text messages. I am practising clear, phone-friendly event flyers and made a sample layout with fictional details. Would a simple event graphic be useful for one upcoming activity? No worries if not.

Ask permission before using someone's logo, photo, name, testimonial or work in a public sample.

## Step 6 · Deliver and learn

Use a short delivery checklist:

1. Confirm the brief and files.
2. Make a first version and check spelling, links, phone layout and export quality.
3. Share it for review through the agreed channel.
4. Apply the included feedback and confirm final approval.
5. Deliver the final files in the promised format.
6. Ask what worked and what could be improved.

Keep copies of your own work, not a client's sensitive information. If a task turns out to be beyond your skills or the agreement, say so early and discuss a fair next step.

## Step 7 · Practise digital safety

- Use a unique password and enable multi-factor authentication where available.
- Never ask a customer to send a password or one-time code.
- Do not upload private documents or personal data to an AI tool without permission and an appropriate privacy review.
- Check links and file sources before downloading.
- Get written permission before accessing or testing a website, account or device.
- Keep a simple record of what was agreed, delivered and paid.

## A seven-day practice challenge

**Day 1:** List three tasks people ask for help with. Choose one.

**Day 2:** Ask a potential user what they need and what a useful result looks like.

**Day 3:** Make one original sample using fictional data.

**Day 4:** Write a one-paragraph offer with a clear deliverable and boundaries.

**Day 5:** Ask a trusted person to review whether the offer is easy to understand.

**Day 6:** Practise one respectful outreach message; do not mass-send it.

**Day 7:** Reflect on the feedback and pick one skill to improve next.

## Reflection

- What did I enjoy doing?
- Which task felt difficult, and what do I need to learn?
- What did a potential user actually say?
- What will I practise before offering this as paid work?
- How will I protect the other person's information and expectations?

**Keep it honest, keep practising, and make useful work visible.**
